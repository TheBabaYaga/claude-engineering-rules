# No AI slop and ASD-STE100 together

`CLAUDE.md` has two prose standards. They cover different problems, and in six places they pull in opposite directions. This file gives the precedence.

## The split

ASD-STE100 fixes the **form** of a sentence: the tense, the voice, the length, the word choice. It cannot tell you if a sentence is worth writing.

No AI slop fixes the **substance** and the **texture**: is the sentence specific, does it earn its place, does it read as machine-written.

Apply both to prose that you write. A sentence can pass every STE rule and still be slop. "The tool improves developer productivity" is active, present tense, 6 words, and plain. It is also portable to any tool in the world, so it is filler.

## Where they agree

Four rules appear in both standards. Apply them once.

| Rule | STE | No AI slop |
|---|---|---|
| One name for one thing | 1.11, 9.4 | Synonym cycling |
| Use the verb, not the noun | 3.7 | Make verbs do the work |
| Active voice | 3.6 | Use active voice |
| Delete the quality adjective | GR-6, checklist item 4 | Importance puffery |

Both standards also protect a hedge. STE protects it as a deliberate departure. No AI slop cuts an empty qualifier only.

## Where they conflict

**1. Voice.** STE flattens voice on purpose. It was built so that a reader with poor English cannot misread an aircraft manual. No AI slop protects voice.

Resolution by owner of the text:

- Prose that you write: STE wins. Flatten it.
- A draft of mine that you edit: no AI slop wins. Never rewrite my words into STE.
- My voice in a Slack message: use the `my-voice` skill. Neither standard applies.

**2. Lists.** STE rule 4.1 wants a vertical list for a sequence of three or more steps. No AI slop calls a bullet list slop when two sentences of prose read better.

Resolution: a list for a real sequence of steps, a real set of options, or a real table of values. Prose for reasoning, for an explanation, and for a comparison of two things.

**3. The em dash.** STE permits it. No AI slop restricts it.

Resolution: the restriction wins. No em dash inside a sentence by default. A label separator in a list or a table is not affected.

**4. Sentence variety.** The STE caps of 20 and 25 words push every sentence toward the same short shape. No AI slop calls that robotic rhythm.

Resolution: keep the cap. Vary the shape inside it. A 6-word sentence next to an 18-word sentence obeys both standards.

**5. Fragments.** STE rule 4.5 wants the articles and complete sentences. No AI slop permits a fragment when it is characteristic of the writer.

Resolution: complete sentences in prose that you write. Keep my fragments in my draft.

**6. The 20-word cap against precision.** Both standards refuse to trade a fact for brevity. STE rule 4.2 says do not remove words to make a sentence shorter. No AI slop says protect the specific fact.

Resolution: no conflict. Keep the longer sentence when the shorter one drops a condition, a scope limit, or a number.

## Order of work

1. Write the substance. Be specific. Apply the portability test.
2. Remove the slop patterns and the banned words.
3. Apply the STE structural rules to what is left.
4. Read it once against the checks in both sections of `CLAUDE.md`.

Step 3 is last for a reason. A clean STE rewrite of an empty paragraph gives you a short, clean, empty paragraph.
