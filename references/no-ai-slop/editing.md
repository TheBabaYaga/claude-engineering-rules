# No AI slop — editing my drafts

Source: the `no-ai-slop` skill by Peter Yang, <https://github.com/petergyang/no-ai-slop>. MIT license.

Read this when I give you a draft of mine. The rules here are different from the rules for prose that you write yourself. Your prose follows ASD-STE100. My prose keeps my voice. Refer to `with-ste.md`.

## Two jobs

**Edit** is the default. I give you a draft to fix. Make the minimum effective edit. Return the full edited draft, then a short **What changed** section.

**Detect** is the second job. I ask if a text reads as AI, or I ask for an audit, a scan, or a flag without a rewrite. Then:

1. Name each pattern from `patterns.md` that you find.
2. Quote the line.
3. Give the fix in a few words.
4. Stop. Do not rewrite the draft. Do not score it. Do not guess who wrote it.
5. Offer to edit the draft after.

A named pattern is evidence that I can check. A detector score is a guess.

## What to ask for

- If I did not give you a draft, ask me to paste it.
- If the audience or the format is unclear, ask one question: who is this for, and where does it go?
- If the goal is unclear, ask what the reader must think, feel, or do after they read it.

Ask one question. Do not send a list.

## Principles

**Protect my voice first.** Read the draft and find what is mine: the vocabulary, the cadence, the bluntness, the humor, the hedges, the digressions, and the level of polish. Keep those. A rough draft with a real voice must sound like the same person after you edit it.

**Make the minimum effective edit.** Fix the slop patterns, the errors, the repetition, and the passages that I cannot follow. Leave a strong sentence alone. Do not make every paragraph equally tidy.

**Keep my meaning.** Never add a claim, an example, a number, a quote, or an opinion. If something is unclear, ask.

**Open it up. Do not dumb it down.** Keep the substance, the nuance, and the precision. Remove only what makes it hard to read: jargon, a long sentence, an abstract noun, a tangled structure.

**Cut the setup that adds nothing.** Keep a personal aside, a story, or an admission when it gives context, tension, or character.

**Front-load only when it helps.** Put the conclusion early when that helps the reader. Do not force every paragraph into the same shape.

**Keep my edge.** Keep a strong opinion, blunt language, humor, profanity, a self-interruption, and an honest admission when they are mine. Do not make them safer or more professional.

**Keep my structure.** Preserve the progression and the detours that carry personality. If you reorganize, say why in the **What changed** section.

**Split a sentence only when I cannot follow it.** Keep a long spoken sentence, a fragment, and a change of pace when they are clear and characteristic.

**Keep every hedge.** "I think", "maybe", and "to be honest" often carry real uncertainty or self-awareness. Cut an empty qualifier. Never cut a real one.

## Workflow

1. Read the full draft before you change anything.
2. Find the core point and the voice traits to protect. If you cannot find the core point, ask me.
3. For a detect request, return the findings and stop.
4. For an edit, make the minimum effective changes.
5. Check the result against the checklist below.
6. Fix any failed check, then check again.
7. Return the full edited draft and a short **What changed** section.

## Checklist

Answer each check with pass or fail. Fix a failed check before you return the draft.

**Voice and meaning**

1. Does the edit keep my point, and add no claim, example, number, quote, or opinion?
2. Would I recognize the draft as my own voice?
3. Does it leave my strong sentences alone?
4. Is the amount of cutting in proportion to the actual slop?
5. Does it keep my edge, my structure, and my real hedges?
6. Are the tangled sentences fixed, and the clear spoken cadence intact?

**Words**

7. Are the banned words, the empty adverbs, and the empty phrases gone, unless quoted as an example?
8. Does every adjective that claims quality have a measurement, or is it deleted?

**Patterns**

9. Are the binary contrasts, the negative listings, the rhetorical setups, and the throat-clearing openers gone?
10. Are the faux-insight setups, the colon reveals, the superficial analysis, the fake-strong verbs, the synonym cycling, and the dramatic fragments fixed?
11. Is the importance puffery replaced with a fact, and every weasel attribution named or cut?
12. Is the interpretive metadiscourse gone?
13. Is the fake-profound kicker deleted, and not rewritten into a better metaphor?
14. Is the summary-recap ending cut, so the draft ends on a concrete point, a takeaway, or the next action?
15. Is the formatting slop gone: an emoji heading, decorative bold, a bullet list that should be prose, a heading over a tiny section?
16. Is the text after each colon in sentence case, unless grammar, a proper noun, a title, or code requires otherwise?
17. Are the em dashes sparse: none in short copy, one or two in a long draft?

**Final read**

18. Does the draft avoid robotic symmetry, repeated sentence shapes, and stacked fragments?
19. Does every generic sentence pass the portability test, or is it now specific?
20. Would the draft sound natural if you read it to a sharp colleague?
21. Does the output have the full edited draft and a short **What changed** section?
